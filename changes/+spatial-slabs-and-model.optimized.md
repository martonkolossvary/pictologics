Moran's I and Geary's C pick the faster of the pair loop and the FFT by a time model, measured at 1 to 10 threads. The FFT runs on one worker when that is faster (a small grid). A sparse ROI of 15,457 voxels in a 72x72x72 box takes 7.9 ms instead of 13.9 ms. A full 16x16x16 ROI takes 0.56 ms instead of 0.61 ms. When the FFT grid does not fit in half of the memory, the FFT runs on slabs of the ROI box, within the memory. Before, the pair loop ran. A thin shell of 519,640 voxels with a 4 GiB limit takes 1.2 s instead of 15 s. A 512x512x300 CT without a mask with a 16 GiB limit takes 5.1 s (12.9 GB) instead of about 100 hours. The values change by at most about 4e-14 (relative). The IBSI values stay the same, bit for bit. The choice uses the thread count of the import, so the values do not depend on `set_num_threads`.
