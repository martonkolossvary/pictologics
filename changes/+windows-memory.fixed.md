On Windows, the FFT of Moran's I and Geary's C assumed 8 GiB of memory. So it used at most 4 GiB, whatever the memory of the computer. Larger ROIs then took the slow loop over the voxel pairs. A thin shell of 519,640 voxels took 15 s instead of 0.6 s. A body ROI of a 512x512x200 CT took about 10 hours instead of 0.9 s. One reader now gives the memory of the computer to the whole package. On Windows it reads `GlobalMemoryStatusEx` and checks its result. On macOS and Linux it reads `sysconf` and checks each number. On Linux it takes at most the memory limit of the cgroup of the process (cgroup v2, then v1). That limit is the smallest limit on its path, for example of a Slurm job. It assumes 8 GiB only when the system gives no valid number. The resample check uses the share of this process: the memory of the computer, divided among the `run_batch` workers. Its message gives GiB.
