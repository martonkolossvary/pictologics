Moran's I and Geary's C of a large ROI take half the time and 30 % less memory. The spectrum of the kernel 1 / |r| is real, so the FFT sums take it from the DCT of its half grid. They get the Moran numerator from the spectrum of the centred intensities, with no inverse transform. One padded buffer serves both forward transforms. The CT lesion at 0.5 mm takes 12.6 ms instead of 25.6 ms at 10 threads. A 2.3 million voxel ROI takes 170 ms instead of 347 ms, with a peak of 1.0 GB instead of 1.5 GB. The values move by at most 1e-13 (relative), in Moran's I of a random image near zero. The IBSI values move by at most 4e-15. Below 450 ROI voxels, the pair loop runs on one thread. A tiny ROI then takes 16 to 42 us instead of 115 to 120 us, with the same values, bit for bit.
