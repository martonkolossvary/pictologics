Moran's I and Geary's C of a large ROI take half the time and 30 % less memory. The spectrum of the kernel 1 / |r| is real, so the FFT sums take it from the DCT of its half grid, get the Moran numerator from the spectrum of the centred intensities (no inverse transform), and fill one padded buffer for both forward transforms. The CT lesion at 0.5 mm takes 12.6 ms instead of 25.6 ms at 10 threads, and a 2.3 million voxel ROI 170 ms instead of 347 ms with a peak of 1.0 GB instead of 1.5 GB. The values move by at most 6e-14 (relative), in Moran's I of a random image near zero; the IBSI values move by at most 4e-15. Below 450 ROI voxels, the pair loop runs on one thread: a tiny ROI takes 16 to 42 us instead of 115 to 120 us, with the same values, bit for bit.
