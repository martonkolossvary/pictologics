Rotation-invariant Laws filters pool each base response as soon as it is ready and free it after its last rotation, instead of keeping every base response plus a signed copy for each of the 24 rotations. Max and min pooling change the sign of a base in place and skip a rotation that repeats a base and sign; average pooling subtracts the base. The separable passes and the energy image write into their own arrays. The values are the same, bit for bit. With the IBSI 2 phase 3 settings (max pooling and energy), a 512×512×200 CT takes 1.27 s instead of 1.82 s and 2.9 GB instead of 5.0 GB; smaller CT images take 20-41% less time and 42% less memory. Min pooling without energy takes 43-57% of the time on images from 64×64×64 voxels up, and a Laws energy filter without rotations needs 57% less memory.
