Filters use less memory. Wavelet passes after the first write into the same array, so a rotation-invariant wavelet on images above 2 million voxels needs 46-64% less memory (Coiflet 3, level 2: 793 MB to 283 MB) and a plain wavelet 67% less. Simoncelli and Riesz filters multiply the image spectrum by the transfer function in place for float64 images and let the inverse FFT reuse that array: Simoncelli takes up to 24% less time and 58% less memory (a 512×512×200 CT: 449 ms to 345 ms, 2,517 MB to 1,049 MB), and Riesz up to 18% less time and 17% less memory. Transfer tables of more than 8 million values are built in slabs, so the first Simoncelli or Riesz filter of a large image peaks at 536 MB or 573 MB instead of 2,569 MB or 1,721 MB. Results are unchanged, bit for bit.
